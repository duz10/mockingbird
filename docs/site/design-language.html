Design Language · v1.0

Mockingbird

A local-first voice-to-text app. Material 3 structure, Liquid Glass surfaces, warm earth-tone palette. Dark mode by default; built to feel alive.

§ 01 · Brand

The mark

Three vertical ellipses tapering down, ending in a play triangle. Voice in, playback out: a waveform that's already moving. The mark renders one of three ways depending on surface; never recolored beyond these treatments.

§ 07 · Motion

Soft start, decisive finish

Mockingbird favors M3's Emphasized easing: slow ease in, brisk ease out. Movement feels considered, never twitchy. Most transitions live in the 150–350ms range; anything longer than 500ms is reserved for state changes the user explicitly initiated.

§ 08 · Logo motion

The mark, alive

Three motion states for the Mockingbird logo. The ellipses are the living element. They expand into being, oscillate while listening, collapse into stillness. The play triangle stays still as the anchor. When the mark is in motion, it is the waveform; no separate indicator needed.

Entry & exit

Ellipses expand vertically from their centers, staggered left to right, voice traveling toward the output. The play triangle scales in last, settling the composition. Total duration ~980ms. Use for app launch, login load, and any "voice is now active" moment. Once per session.

Active loop

Each ellipse oscillates on its own period (1.35s, 1.65s, 1.15s) and starts at a different phase. The mismatched timings prevent a synchronized pulse and give the "real voice" irregularity. The triangle stays still: the steady output the energy flows toward.

Loop · ease-in-out · 1.15–1.65s · offset phases ● Live

Idle ↔ active

When Mockingbird is idle, the ellipses collapse to circles, quiet and waiting. The play triangle stays full size as the anchor. When recording begins, the ellipses extend back to full height (500ms transition) and the active loop takes over.

State · Idle

When to use each

Splash entry

App launch, login completion, onboarding step transitions, "voice is now ready" moments. Once per session; not for ambient state changes.

Idle state

Listening pill at rest, menu bar icon, anywhere the logo appears in an interactive surface that could become active.

Active loop

While recording, real-time transcription in progress, wherever the user needs unambiguous "yes, audio is being captured" confirmation.

Static

Marketing surfaces, documentation, app icon, anywhere the logo represents the brand rather than a state. Static is the default; animate with intent.

Accessibility · reduced motion

All three motions respect prefers-reduced-motion. With reduced-motion enabled, splash collapses to a 200ms fade, the active loop stops oscillating, and the listening pill falls back to the green dot pulse alone. All of it is already wired into this page's CSS.

§ 12 · Voice & tone

Plainspoken, with warmth

Mockingbird speaks like someone who actually uses the app: precise about what it does, honest about what it doesn't, never marketing-y. Direct, never curt. Privacy is mentioned plainly, not performed. The serif headlines do the warmth; the copy does the work.

01
Say what it does, not how it makes you feel

"Transcribes on-device" beats "frictionless dictation experience." If a sentence could be a SaaS landing page in any other industry, rewrite it.

02
Privacy as fact, not feature

"Audio stays on this device" is true. "Bank-grade encryption" is theater. Mockingbird doesn't sell privacy; it just happens to be private because of how it's built.

03
Errors don't apologize

No "Oops!", no "Something went wrong." State the problem and the next action. "Microphone disconnected. Reconnect and tap Resume."

04
Numbers in mono

Durations, word counts, timestamps, hotkeys: always IBM Plex Mono. It's a small thing that signals precision throughout the app.
